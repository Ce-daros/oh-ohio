# Two city days, two different rhythms

*Cincinnati is a compact streetcar-and-walk day. Dayton’s aviation story stretches across separate sites, so plan it in sections and leave room for transport and rest.*

Cincinnati’s central loop makes it possible to choose one stop at a time. Dayton’s aviation sites are dispersed across downtown, a residential neighborhood, and the Wright-Patterson area, so plan the day in sections.

## Cincinnati: market, park, music

**Short version (about half a day):** begin at Findlay Market, then take the Cincinnati Connector from either Findlay Market station to Washington Park. Stay for a seated break and a walk through the park, then continue downtown if you have time. Check the market’s current calendar before planning a fixed meal or visit.

**Longer version (a full, flexible day):** start at the market. Board at Findlay Market–Race (station 12), then leave the streetcar at Washington Park (station 14). From there, continue along the posted station order through the Public Library and Aronoff Center to Fountain Square, then The Banks. Use the park and riverfront as longer rests; one or two stops can fill the day without riding the whole 3.6-mile loop.

**Getting around and access:** the city says the Connector is fare-free and has level boarding, audio and visual stop information, and spaces for wheelchairs and scooters. The city’s accessibility page points riders to Metro Access, a shared-ride service for people whose disabilities might prevent them from riding fixed-route service. Metro’s service area and operating hours are limited; eligibility and visitor arrangements must be confirmed with Metro in advance. A level vehicle does not make every sidewalk, curb, market aisle, or destination entrance step-free, so check those places separately. For a walking-light day, ride between stations and spend longer at one destination; for more walking, pair market browsing with the short Over-the-Rhine park walk.

**Route diagram**

`Findlay Market–Race (#12) → Liberty & Race (#13) → Washington Park (#14) → Public Library (#16) → Fountain Square (#18) → The Banks (#1)`

Station numbers and order follow the city’s published route map. Check current service alerts before setting out.

## Dayton: Dunbar, Wrights, and the space between

Dayton’s history asks for more than one stop. The National Park Service (NPS) ties together Paul Laurence Dunbar, Wilbur and Orville Wright, neighborhood life, and aviation, but its sites are geographically separate. Begin at the Wright-Dunbar Interpretive Center, 16 South Williams Street. The center introduces both Dunbar and the Wrights; the adjoining bicycle shop is directly next door. The NPS says the nearest Greater Dayton RTA stop is at West Third and Williams. This makes a focused downtown visit the most realistic transit-first plan: visit the center and shop, pause in the neighborhood, then decide whether to continue.

**Short version (2–3 hours):** see the Wright-Dunbar center and Wright Cycle Company shop, then rest before returning to your stop. If the Paul Laurence Dunbar House is open on your date, NPS walking directions go three blocks west along Third Street and three blocks north on Paul Laurence Dunbar Street. Confirm hours and tours first; schedules differ by site. The park accessibility page documents an elevator, accessible restrooms, benches, a ramp into the Cycle Company, captions and audio description for the film, and typed conversation equipment at the visitor center. It offers indoor seating and interpretation in one place.

**Longer version (a full day, with a car or arranged ride):** spend the morning at Wright-Dunbar and Dunbar House, eat and rest downtown, then travel separately to the Huffman Prairie Flying Field Interpretive Center and the National Museum of the U.S. Air Force. NPS says there is no public transportation to the Huffman Prairie center. Use Greater Dayton RTA’s trip planner for the bus portion to Wright-Dunbar; plan another ride for the outlying sites. An alternate full day is to stay downtown and add a longer neighborhood visit rather than hurry between far-apart locations.

**Route diagram**

`West Third & Williams stop → Wright-Dunbar Interpretive Center (16 S Williams St) → Wright Cycle Company (22 S Williams St) → Dunbar House (219 N Paul Laurence Dunbar St; 3 blocks west + 3 north) → [separate ride] Huffman Prairie (2380 Memorial Rd) / Air Force Museum`

For both cities, choose one anchor and one optional stop before adding more. Carry water and allow time to sit. Transit status, park hours, event schedules, and accessible vehicle arrangements change; check the operators for your travel date.
