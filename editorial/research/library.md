## Publishable draft

### One title to look for

Let’s follow one book instead of trying to learn an entire library system at once. Try searching your local public library catalog for Alyssa McClanahan’s *Findlay Market of Cincinnati: A History* (Arcadia Publishing, 2021; ISBN 9781467148597). It is a focused history of the Cincinnati market and its place in the city. The title gives us a practical question: if the book is not on the shelf at your branch, can your library request it from another Ohio collection?

The answer depends on the library that issued your card and whether a lending copy is available. OhioLINK says its shared collections now appear through participating local library catalogs; it retired the Central Catalog as a standalone service in June 2025. SearchOhio and OhioLINK updated their requesting technology in fall 2025, and OhioLINK reports that lending resumed in May 2026. So an old bookmarked catalog or an older instruction sheet may lead to the wrong screen. Begin with the current catalog linked from your own library.

### Place the request from your library

Search the title, author, or ISBN. If your library’s catalog shows SearchOhio or OhioLINK holdings, open the record and choose its request option. SearchOhio’s current patron guide describes the steps: select your affiliated library, sign in with your last name and library card number (and PIN if that library requires one), then choose a pickup location. That request is made through your library’s access and account rules. If the record does not appear, a librarian can help check the title and possible interlibrary-loan routes; the public catalog does not guarantee that a copy can be borrowed.

The statewide SearchOhio guide says requests are usually ready in three to five business days, except for weekend requests, and stay on the pickup shelf for ten days. Treat that as an estimate, not a delivery promise. A lending library may be closed, a copy may already be requested, or a particular title may not circulate. The local library’s notice when the item arrives is the useful signal to plan around.

For a concrete local rule set, Columbus Metropolitan Library currently lists 21 days for SearchOhio print materials, audiobooks, and music; movies have a seven-day loan. It lists up to three renewals when no other request is waiting and a ten-day pickup hold. Its page also says SearchOhio and OhioLINK have separate fee and replacement rules from ordinary CML materials. Those are Columbus rules, not statewide terms. Another library may set different eligibility, loan, renewal, and replacement conditions, so check the page for the library that issued your card and the due date printed on the item or account.

### Return it close to home

When the book is ready, pick it up at the location selected with the request. When you finish, the item’s owning library does not require a special trip: Columbus Metropolitan Library says SearchOhio and OhioLINK items can be returned to any CML location or a CLC partner library. That return information is specific to CML. Other participating libraries publish their own return instructions, so follow the rules on your account rather than assuming every system shares identical drop-off arrangements.

Then let the book change how you look at the place it describes. Findlay Market’s own history traces its market house to 1852 and explains that contractor disputes delayed opening until 1855. A book can add residents, merchants, and changing city life to that building timeline. After reading, compare a passage with the market’s public history and its historic photographs or records. Keep the book’s author, the institution’s account, and the archival object visible as separate sources.

That is the nice scale of a library request: one title, one card, one pickup branch, one return. It is also a small example of shared public infrastructure. A copy travels across the state because libraries agree to lend to one another; for the reader, the trip begins in a familiar catalog and ends at a familiar desk. Search for the book, check what your library offers, and ask for help if the request path is unclear.

## Research notes, sourcing, and limits

- [OhioLINK: Resources](https://www.ohiolink.edu/content/ohiolink_resources) — opened 2026-09-25. Supports shared print access through local library catalogs, the June 26, 2025 end of the standalone Central Catalog, and SearchOhio system update with lending resumed May 2026.
- [SearchOhio: Using SearchOhio](https://searchohio.org/searchohio/index.php/using-searchohio/) — opened 2026-09-25. Supports the current request sequence, authentication fields, pickup selection, stated 3–5 business day estimate, and 10-day hold shelf.
- [Columbus Metropolitan Library: SearchOhio & OhioLINK Catalogs](https://www.columbuslibrary.org/searchohio/) — opened 2026-09-25. Supports Columbus-specific SearchOhio loan and renewal periods, pickup hold, return options, and notice that loan/fee/replacement policies vary from regular CML items.
- [OhioLINK: Frequently Asked Questions](https://www.ohiolink.edu/content/frequently_asked_questions) — opened 2026-09-25. Supports public-library card eligibility for borrowing circulating OhioLINK materials through SearchOhio libraries and clarifies that the local library is the right point of contact for requests and fines.
- [Arcadia Publishing: Findlay Market of Cincinnati](https://www.arcadiapublishing.com/collections/american-palate/state-ohio) — opened 2026-09-25. Supports the bibliographic identity and publisher description of McClanahan’s 2021 market history. It does not establish that a particular local or OhioLINK copy is available to borrow.
- [Findlay Market: History](https://www.findlaymarket.org/history) — opened 2026-09-25. Supports the market timeline used in the final section.
